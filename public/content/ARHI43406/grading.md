### Course requirements

The primary means of evaluation will be presentations and papers, but preparation and participation will weigh heavily in your final grade as well.

**Papers and other written assignments**  
There are two kinds of writing assignments:

* **Short paragraphs & expert days** – Nearly every class you will email me (as a running Google Doc with newest entry at top, *before* class) a two‑paragraph response to the readings:
  * summarize the author’s main argument in your own words as the argument pertains to class and the topic for the day  
  * pose two follow‑up questions.  These responses foster engagement and launch discussion.  
  * On your assigned “expert” days you will also give a brief visual presentation connecting key ideas to an artwork. 
  * Pass / Revise / Fail grading, worth **25 %** of the final grade.  
  * No late paragraphs accepted.  
  * You may skip **three** responses per semester at any time (note “skip” on those dates).
  
* **Annotated bibliography** – One assignment supporting your final paper. **7 %** of the final grade.

* **Research paper** – One 11–12‑page paper (**35 %**):
  * Meet with me in November; rough drafts strongly encouraged.  
  * Outlines & bibliographies due on the syllabus date.  
  * Late work loses one‑half letter grade per day (extensions possible if requested by specified date).

  * **Presentation** – A 15‑minute talk based on resesarch for your paper, with images and outline. **8 %** of the final grade.

* **Presentations & participation** – Seminar attendance and active, informed discussion are required.  
  * Up to **2** unexcused absences allowed; beyond that, deductions apply.  
  * Participation + attendance = **25 %** of the final grade.  
  * On “expert” days you lead discussion (more to come); on other days please arrive ready to contribute.

### Honor Code and AI Policy
All assignments are required; failure to complete any assignment can result in failure of the course.  
Notre Dame’s Academic Code of Honor applies. *No generative‑AI tools (e.g., ChatGPT, Grammarly) may be used on any assignment.* First offense = failing the assignment without revision; second offense = failing the course.
