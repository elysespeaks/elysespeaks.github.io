## Requirements

* **Quiz:** a short (announced) quiz will be given early in the semester to give you a sense of the exam format **(5 %)**
* **Exams:**  
  &nbsp;&nbsp;• *exam 1* — **25 %**  
  &nbsp;&nbsp;• *exam 2* (not comprehensive) — **35 %**
* **Preparation, participation, and attendance:** both are mandatory and worth a large percentage of your final grade **(15 %)**. One excused absence is permitted; after that a 3 % deduction will occur from the participation grade for each absence unless there are extenuating circumstances. Please discuss all absences with me. Five or more absences will fully impair the ability to pass this course in nearly all cases.  

  > **A note on preparation and attendance**  
  > Coming to class is a good start on the road to good participation, but preparation (i.e., reading) and informed verbal participation are necessary for achieving success in the course. You will need to read *and* to talk in order to be seen as an excellent participant in the course.  
  >  
  > When you contribute to class discussion, we all benefit from questions and comments that are informed and meaningful. This makes class much more interesting for everyone! Because of this, you will be expected not only to contribute to class, but to contribute in ways that make it clear that you are familiar with, and fully engaged with the course materials. However, *there are no stupid questions in this class!* Often the most basic questions and observations are the most interesting. If you have trouble contributing in class, please see me to discuss alternate methods for participation.

* **Writing assignments:** You will be asked to visit the Raclin Murphy Museum this semester to choose a work on which to write an essay **(20 %)**. Late assignments are accepted if a valid request is made at least 48 hours in advance; otherwise they will receive a 3‑point deduction per day.

> *All assignments, exams, and papers are subject to the academic honor code.*

### **Honor Code Policy and Use of AI**

Each assignment is required, in the sense that failure to complete one or more assignments is sufficient to fail the course. The Notre Dame Academic Code of Honor Pledge expects students to abide by it:

> “As a member of the Notre Dame community, I will not participate in or tolerate academic dishonesty.”

This includes the use of generative AI tools (including Grammarly and similar grammar tools). For the purposes of this class, you may **not** engage in unauthorized collaboration to complete any work for the course, and you may **not** use any generative‑AI composition or editing tools. Please visit the [honor code website](https://honorcode.nd.edu/) if you have any questions about its terms and conditions.

There is a website for this course, and it should be your first resource. Check it often, as assignments, handouts, and other materials will appear on it. The exams will be based partly on image identification and comparison/analysis. If you have never taken a *slide test* before, be prepared! They require consistent practice and regular study throughout the semester. This means logging on often to review the image lists and familiarize yourself with the materials.
