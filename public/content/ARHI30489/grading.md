N.B. This is a writing intensive course.

- **Short annotations and expert days**: you will be expected to respond to all readings each day before class in a 3-paragraph essay (about one page). YOU CAN SKIP UP TO THREE DAYS OF READINGS BY NOTING "SKIP" in your google doc. These should be created in a running document on Google Docs (with the newest entry at the top), single-spaced with the date and topic at the top, and sent to me by email **each day before class. No late entries will be permitted unless discussed in advance due to an excused absence.** These are meant to 1) promote active engagement with the reading and 2) act as a means to generate discussion questions and knowledge. Again, you may skip up to two per semester, and on a couple of days these will not be required. The paragraphs will be graded (check; check plus; check minus-which requires a rewrite) together with your participation in acting as the "expert" in discussion for the day. Everyone is scheduled for one expert day - days in which you'll kick off class with a short presentation and discussion. Total 15% of your grade.
- **Visual analysis papers**: there will be 2 longer papers -- 1 4-5 pp. paper and 1 9-10 pp. final paper. Late penalties of 5% points off assessed per day unless an extension was granted in advance. These will count for 25 and 30% of your grade.
  - Presentation: each student will also give an 10-minute final presentation based on their final research papers. This will be an in-depth research presentation that should include images. These are worth 7% of your final grade.
- **Annotated bibliography**: you will construct one annotated bibliography in preparation for your final research papers. To be discussed in class - worth 8% of your grade.
- **Museum visit**: You will be expected to go to the AIC at least twice during the semester, ideally once toward the beginning, and then again together as a class. We will take a class field trip near the end of the semester (date TBA). Your paper assignments will relate to a work of your choice at the AIC but must be approved by me. Missing one or both of these trips will result in a deduction from your participation grade.
- **Preparation and participation**: As this is a seminar, attendance is required. A maximum of 2 unexcused absences will be accepted (talk to me in advance if you need to miss class, or bring a doctor's note if you cannot contact me in advance). Participation (by way of intelligent contributions to class discussion of the readings and works of art) is essential to the course, so together with attendance this will account for 15% of your final grade.

<strong>All</strong>strong> assignments, exams, and papers are subject to the academic honor code. Generative artificial intelligence (AI) programs, such as ChatGPT, may not be used for any work or assignments required in this course. The use of generative AI programs defeats the writing requirements and critical thinking skills that are vital to achieving our learning outcomes. Submission of partial or complete work from generative AI programs is not permitted and will be treated as plagiarism as defined within the academic honor code.

Below is a rough guide to how participation is assessed (thanks to Chris Moorman at the Fuqua School):

- **Outstanding Contributor**: Contributions in class reflect exceptional preparation. Ideas are nearly always important and provide one or more major insights as well as providing a fruitful direction for the class. Arguments are well substantiated and persuasively presented.
- **Good Contributor**: Contributions in class reflect thorough preparation. Ideas are usually important, provide good insights, and sometimes offer a fruitful direction for the class. Arguments are generally well substantiated and are often persuasive.
- **Adequate Contributor**: Contributions in class reflect satisfactory preparation. Ideas are sometimes important, provide generally useful insights, but seldom offer a major new direction. Arguments are fairly well-substantiated and sometimes persuasive.
- **Unsatisfactory Contributor**: Contributions in class reflect inadequate preparation. Ideas offered are seldom substantive; they provide few, if any, insights; and never offer a constructive direction for the class. Integrative comments are absent. Class contributions are, at best, "cherry-picking" efforts making isolated, obvious, or confusing points. If this person were not a member of the class, valuable air time would be saved.
- **Non-participant**: The student was present but said nothing or the student missed class. Hence, there is not adequate basis for evaluation.
