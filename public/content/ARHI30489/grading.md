### Short annotations and expert days — 20 %

* You must respond to every reading **before class** in a two‑paragraph essay stored in a running Google Doc (newest entry at the top) and emailed to me each day before class.  
  * You may **skip up to two** days of readings by typing **“SKIP”** in the doc.  
  * Late entries are not accepted without an excused absence arranged in advance.
* Purpose:  
  1. Promote active engagement with the reading.  
  2. Generate discussion questions and shared knowledge.
* Essays are graded **check / check‑plus / check‑minus** (a check‑minus requires a rewrite) and combined with your performance as that day’s **expert** (each student has one scheduled expert day with a short presentation).

---

### Visual‑analysis papers — 55 %

* **Paper 1:** 4‑5 pages (25 %)  
* **Final paper:** 9‑10 pages (30 %)  
  * Late penalty: –5 percentage points per day unless an extension is granted in advance.
* **Final presentation:** 10‑minute research talk with images (5 %).

### Annotated bibliography — 5 %

* Constructed in preparation for the final paper; details in class.

### Museum visit — part of participation

* You must visit the Art Institute of Chicago **twice**:  
  * Once on your own (early in the term).  
  * Once on our class field trip (date TBA).  
* Your papers must focus on an AIC work approved by me.  
* Missing a trip lowers the participation grade.

### Preparation and participation — 15 %

Attendance is required; you may have up to **two unexcused absences** (notify me in advance or bring a doctor’s note). Participation means substantive, informed contributions to discussion.

## Academic honor code and AI

All assignments are subject to the Notre Dame Academic Code of Honor (“I will not participate in or tolerate academic dishonesty”).  
Generative‑AI tools (e.g., ChatGPT, Grammarly) **may not** be used for any work in this course; submitting AI‑generated material is plagiarism under the code.

---

### Participation rubric (adapted from Chris Moorman, Fuqua School)

* **Outstanding contributor** – exceptional preparation; ideas provide major insights and guide the discussion.  
* **Good contributor** – thorough preparation; ideas usually insightful and often advance discussion.  
* **Adequate contributor** – satisfactory preparation; ideas sometimes useful but seldom change direction.  
* **Unsatisfactory contributor** – inadequate preparation; ideas seldom substantive and rarely helpful.  
* **Non‑participant** – present but silent, or absent.
