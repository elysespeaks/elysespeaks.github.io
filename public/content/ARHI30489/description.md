### Topic

This writing‑intensive course will take as its starting point nearby art collections and exhibitions in Chicago and the surrounding areas. This semester we will look primarily at paintings, sculptures, and installations at the Art Institute of Chicago. Drawing heavily on its strong holdings in modern and contemporary European and American art, each class session will be devoted to the careful research and analysis of one or two objects in the collection. We will read art‑historical texts that contextualize each work, allowing us to practice engagement at the visual, critical, and art‑historical levels. We will focus on looking at and writing on visual subjects, so discussion, writing, and participation will be essential components of the course. Some of the artists on whom we will focus include **Monet, Cassatt, Matisse, Mondrian, O’Keeffe, Pollock, Hesse,** and **Warhol**. At least one trip to Chicago will be a required part of the course.

### Format

Attendance is required; some form of written excuse is needed after **2** absences unless discussed with me in advance. The course will be conducted as a seminar, so active participation is necessary. Remember, *there are no silly questions in this class* and sometimes the most basic questions and observations are the most interesting.

### Goals and objectives

* To be visually and critically informed about a part of the collection of the Art Institute of Chicago.  
* To be able to identify and describe key historical periods and styles in modern art.  
* To understand and critically explain the context and circumstances that precipitated the changes that took place across the 19th and 20th centuries in art.  
* To use that information to construct and critique visual and historical arguments about art by:  
  * Taking a position on an issue related to visual subjects.  
  * Raising and answering arguments related to primary and secondary sources on art and art history.  
  * Writing effectively on art‑historical topics.
