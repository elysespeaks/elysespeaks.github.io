### Topic

Art and visual culture have shaped our conceptions of ourselves and others. This course examines contemporary art in a variety of media in order to understand how art contributes to, reflects, affirms, or critiques specific stereotypes of roles and values. It features work and theoretical writing engaged with issues related to identity in all its forms. Some topics include standards of beauty; gender and sexuality; race and identity; performance and multiculturalism; and class, social justice, and ethics. Many of the objects we examine explicitly challenge conventional notions of “good” art, so aesthetic standards of value will be a key topic as well. No specific prerequisites are necessary, but it is useful to have some background in cultural studies, art, or visual culture. Assignments include exams and short papers. **Fulfills the university art and literature requirement. 3 credits**

### Format

Attendance is required in this class; some form of written excuse is needed after **2** absences. The course will be conducted as part lecture, part discussion, so interruption and dialogue during any “lecture” portions are encouraged. Participation in the form of thoughts, questions, and visual observations is strongly encouraged. Please be advised that I do tend to call on students randomly at times, to encourage diversity among voices and to assist you in gaining comfort in using your voices. *There are no stupid questions in this class*, and sometimes the most basic questions and observations are the most interesting. If you are uncomfortable with this policy, email me or speak privately and we can work out an alternate arrangement.

**Please:**

* Be on time — I will take attendance at the beginning of each class and you need to be present when I am doing so.  
* Be respectful of your fellow classmates and me by using your time in class to focus on class. Turn off your phones and *refrain from texting during class*; it is very distracting.  
* Be sure that you come to class awake and ready to talk! I frequently call on students, so please be prepared — bring coffee if need be.  
* Recognize that this is a difficult course that requires attendance *and* attention.  
* Please know that the material in this course can be graphic at times. Contemporary art often takes this approach, so it is unavoidable. If you think you might be offended or made uncomfortable by explicit content, speak to me before committing to the course.  

### **Goals and objectives — after successfully completing this course you should be able to:**

* describe recent examples of contemporary artistic practices.  
* describe recent ways in which contemporary art challenges and responds critically to art history.  
* understand different approaches to art history and visual subjects.  
* recognize ways in which visual culture shapes and affects our perceptions of self and other.  
* understand the idea of cultural constructions of difference.  
* understand and describe arguments for recent challenges to traditional artistic formats, media, and standards.  
* look critically and take an informed approach to visual subjects.  
* think critically and articulate thoughts about visual subjects.
