### Topic

*First-year students only.* 

In this seminar we study and write about a wide range of works of art in local museums. Focus objects span the history of art and include painting, photography, sculpture, prints and more, produced in the Americas, Africa, and Europe. Class meetings center on close looking, historical context, and discussion of readings and writing assignments. Written work consists of several short papers, an oral exam and a longer research paper and mock exhibition submitted at semester’s end.  
**Credits:** 3

### Learning Goals  

- Gain knowledge across the history of art 
- Develop skills in critical writing and visual analysis
- Practice speaking about visual subjects in an academic setting  
- Engage in lively, respectful seminar dialogue  
- Explain recent ways in which exhibitions can challenge and respond critically to art history.
- Understand different approaches to art history and visual subjects.
- Recognize ways in which visual culture shapes and affects values, attitudes, and social ideals.

A further note on assignments: Because looking is one of the key skills to be developed in this course, the assignments will be based entirely around images, and in large part will rely on information only available in class. The *analytic* components that we discuss in class are key to thinking about the work. **Taking good notes is essential to succeeding in this course and I will be looking over/assigning credit to your note taking (this is based on quality, not quanity).** And the readings supplement, but do not cover, all of course content. So when you are taking notes, concentrate on the kinds of **descriptions and analysis** (description alone is not enough!) of the works of art we focus on in class. Learning how to do this kind of visual analysis for yourselves should be your primary goal in preparing for your assignments. Other key components to success in this class include doing the readings before class, asking questions, and attending office hours.
